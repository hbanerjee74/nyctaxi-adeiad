## Declaration versus executable

- **Checked for shape only:** the ontology document, the three mapping documents, the mappings' `ontology_ref` and `semantic_model_ref`, every `concept`/`relationship` name and every `dataset.field` expression.
- **Read by nothing:** every mapping expression. No Studio tool, no dbt, no converter and no upstream parser evaluates one. `vibedata/table-unjoined` and the mapping docs are the only trace a mapping has today.
- **Not proven:** that `trips.trip_key` is unique, that `zone.zone_natural_key` is the TLC location id rather than something that merely looks like it, and that the fleet document's shift grain is what the join assumes. Those are data questions the structural gate cannot ask.
- Therefore: a green `refs` run means the references resolve, never that a mapping works.

## Identity ambiguities found

| Finding | Evidence | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Zone is versioned, so its key is not its identity | `zone_key` is a surrogate over `zone_natural_key` plus valid-from; `trips.pickup_zone_key`/`dropoff_zone_key` carry the surrogate | `Zone` identity maps from `zone.zone_natural_key`. The Trip→Zone relationships are declared and deliberately unmapped, and the trips mapping says why. Mapping them would assert that a version of a zone is the zone. |
| The vendor has the same shape | `vendor_key` is a surrogate over `vendor_natural_key` plus valid-from | Same treatment: `Technology vendor` identity maps from `vendor.vendor_natural_key`, and Trip→vendor stays unmapped. |
| Trip identity is a composite the model now calls a key | `trip_key` is generated by `trip_natural_key` over the recorded columns | Accepted as identity because the macro is the single definition and the model has one declared key, but it is a derived key, not a column the business types. A composite `identify_by` would need a value type per component and is out of scope for this spike. |
| Shift attribution is sparse | `is_fleet_attributed`; `trips.shift_id` is null for most trips | `Trip → Shift` is `ManyToOne` and legitimately absent for unattributed trips: the relationship means "may be attributed to", which the `verbalizes` line says. |
| A medallion and a vehicle claim the same identity | `CONTEXT.md` "Medallion: … and by extension the vehicle it covers"; `dim_vehicle.medallion_number` | No `Medallion` concept. Two concepts would assert two objects over one column. Recorded as a finding: if the business separates the licence from the vehicle, the licence gets a concept and the vehicle a relationship to it. |
| A driver is identified by the operator, not by a person | `driver.driver_key` is the operator's `driver_id`; no licence or national id is exposed | `Driver` identity is the operator's id, and the glossary now says so. Two operators' drivers are not comparable, which is why fleet attribution is per operator. |
| Multiword glossary terms force multiword concept names | `Technology vendor` is the term, so the concept is `Technology vendor`, `verbalizes` included | The glossary gate compares the concept name to the term, so a concept cannot be `TechnologyVendor` while the term stays two words. Worth a line in the Studio design: rename the term or accept the space. |

## The 30 terms with no concept

`glossary` reports them; the review classifies each. None is a rule the ontology should encode — that is what the ADR is for.

| Class | Terms | Why no concept |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Version and time | Zone version, Week, Reporting window | The ontology has no valid-time; periods are not entities. |
| Rule or population | Billable trip, Refund, Zero-distance trip, Published flow, Fleet attribution, Unresolved zone, No-charge and dispute payments | A predicate over trips, zones or payments, not a thing with identity. |
| Measure | Measurable tip rate, Unmeasured tip, Fare residual, Occupancy, Online hour, Locatable idle time, Asset utilisation, Maintenance downtime | A number derived from facts; a metric in the canonical model. |
| Calculated set | Origin-destination flow | A set of trips between two zones, not an object. |
| Classification | Service type, Payment method, Rate plan, Lease type, Daypart, Fare component, Trip band, Trip quality verdict | A code a fact carries, or a category it falls in. The model has dimension tables for several; if one becomes a business object with its own identity and lifecycle, it gets a concept then. |
| Derived interval | Status interval | A row derived from driver status events; its `interval_id` is a row identifier, not a business identity. |
| Deferred identity | Medallion | Argues with `Vehicle` for the same identity; see above. |

`R-07` is accepted on that reading: zero untraced concepts, and no unrepresented term that denotes an object with its own identity, with `Medallion` recorded as the one term where the answer is a business decision rather than a review call.



## Business questions the mappings answer

Four questions a consumer asks across documents, and what the ontology plus its mappings settle. Each answer is a declaration a reviewer can check against the named file; none of them executes.

| Question | Answer the mappings give | Where to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Is the `Zone` in the trips, fleet and zone-POI documents one object? | Yes. One concept, three documents, each mapping its own field: `zone.zone_natural_key` in two and `zone_poi.zone_natural_key` in the third. No semantic-model document can state this, which is the reason the ontology exists. | `ontology/nyc_taxi.ontology.yaml` (`Zone`); the three mapping files |
| Is a trip attributed to a driver, or to a shift? | To a shift. `Trip → Shift` is the only fleet link the fact carries; reach a driver or a vehicle through the shift. A per-driver metric is therefore a two-hop question, not a column. | `Trip.shift` and `Shift.driver` / `Shift.vehicle`; `trips.shift_id` is null for unattributed trips |
| Which version of a zone does a trip earn against? | The ontology does not say, on purpose: a trip's key is the version surrogate, while the concept is the zone across versions. The choice of version is a rule in the model's SCD and its ADR, and the mapping records the gap instead of pretending to answer it. | `Zone.zone_id`; the trips mapping's description; ADR 0015 |
| Does one driver id mean the same thing in the trips document and the fleet document? | Yes. Both map the operator's `driver_id`, so a cross-document join on it is defensible; the glossary now says the id is the operator's, not a national one. | `Driver.driver_id`; `trips.shift_id` → `shift.driver_key` and `driver.driver_key` |

## A second repo, as a completeness test

A subagent was given a bare repo — a `CONTEXT.md` glossary, seven dims, one fact, one aggregate and no canonical artifacts — with these skills, and told to author both artifacts. It produced two model documents and one ontology plus a mapping, and every gate below exited 0.

| Measure | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Gold marts named (`coverage`) | 8 of 8, 0 coverage errors |
| Columns declared | 44 of 46; the two omissions carry a refusal `note` in the model (`dim_shift.online_seconds`, `dim_driver.hire_date`) |
| Metrics declared | 4, one pair per document, aggregate metrics named for their population |
| SCD, hierarchy, role alias | Zone declared SCD2 with a borough hierarchy; the dropoff role declared as an `ALIAS` handle over the same table |
| Glossary terms with a concept | 6 of 9; the three omitted (Zone version, Lease type, Billable trip) are a version, a classification and a rule, and the agent classified each rather than inventing one |
| Relationships mapped | 15 of 19; the four unmapped are one cause — the two zone identity components and the two zone links, all through a version surrogate |
| Declared relationships landing on a key | 8 of 9: `to-column-not-a-key` warned that the trip-to-vendor join uses the vendor's natural key while the dimension's key is a version surrogate |

Three gaps, in order of what they cost a reader:

1. **The vendor dimension has no concept.** The model declares `dim_vendor` with SCD history, and the glossary has no Vendor term, so the agent had to use `vendor_id` as a bare identity value of `Trip`. Every rule was followed — the ontology never invents vocabulary — and the pair is still incomplete by one dimension. The fix is one change: add the term and the concept together.
2. **Trip's identity is mapped four of six components.** Correct on the data, and reported, but the mapping is a partial identity and nothing said so until `mapping/partial-identity` existed.
3. **A join to a versioned dimension has no as-of expression.** `to-column-not-a-key` named it; the agent described it as expected rather than resolving it, which leaves the vendor join ambiguous about which version a trip saw.

The trial found one hole in this repo's gate: before `e8bd26c`, a mapping stripped to one of Trip's six identity components still exited 0 with no error and no warning. `mapping/partial-identity` now reports it, and the same trial is what a Domain inheriting these skills should expect to reproduce — structurally complete, with the semantic gaps named.


## A mart change, as a second lifecycle test

The same repo was then given a mart change by another engineer: `dim_vehicle` became Type 2 and a new `agg_driver_daily` appeared. Two runs, one before these lifecycle rules and one after, both landed it correctly — model document re-derived, `Vehicle` identity moved from the version surrogate to the natural key, a mapping added for the new document, a **Vehicle version** term added to the glossary. The rules were not what made that happen; the agent read the changed SQL and the gates told it where to look.

So the pass hardened two observed gaps rather than fixing a demonstrated one:

- `notes` reported `0 dangling pointer(s)` over a repo with no tests or ADR directory — a clean result that proved nothing. It now warns `notes/no-directory` and the gate stays green, because the directories are the repo's to create.
- `coverage`'s root was described as "the path `repo-map.json` gives the canonical model's layer", which is not a key. It now names `layers.<name>.path`, falling back to the parent of `artifact_paths.semantic_model`.

The ontology-update rule (a changed dataset re-derives its mapping; an identity move takes the concept, its glossary sentence and every mapping together) is preventative: the failure it prevents is a mapping that still resolves to a real field while identifying something else, and neither run produced one. It is recorded as a `workarounds` rule and a `skill-changes` row for `generating-dbt-model`, which is where it belongs once the plugin carries it.

## Go / no-go

**Go**, with the declaration limit stated wherever the ontology is described.

The mechanism does what the spike set out to show: one ontology holds an object once, three per-document mappings point at it, the identity of a versioned dimension is decided once and applied everywhere, and the gate catches the failures that a plausible-looking mapping hides (an identity that is really a surrogate, a concept with no glossary term, a document nobody mapped).

Follow-up scope, in the order it pays:

1. VD-7139 carries the convention: seed the `ontology` skill, the ontology passes in the gate, onboarding's step, the gates in `install-ci`, and the mapping obligation in `generating-dbt-model`.
2. The Studio design gains the multiword-concept-name line and the binary-relationship wording (two roles are two relationships to one concept), so an author does not have to rediscover either.
3. Mapping execution stays out until upstream ships a parser. Nothing here should be described as a working join until then.
